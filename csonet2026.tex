\begin{abstract}
We present a network-aware log anomaly detection pipeline that treats Drain3 templates as vertices of a directed transition graph and each log sequence as a walk on that graph. Walks are scored with explicit transition-probability features (GraphWalk) alongside PCA/SVD, Isolation Forest, a DeepLog-style LSTM, TCN, and a masked Transformer. Classical frequency models remain competitive on HDFS; TCN and DeepLog-style LSTM are nearly tied on BGL; on HUFLIT-Career, compact-vocabulary outlier models remain competitive under heuristic/weak labels. This paper adds GraphWalk scoring, HUFLIT-Career, leakage-aware splits, and deployment analyses beyond a prior HDFS/BGL study.

\keywords{Log anomaly detection \and event-transition network \and Transformer \and Drain3 \and AIOps.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Modern infrastructures generate diverse logs that capture operational state and behavioral patterns. Rule-based detectors age poorly; effective detection requires sequence-level analysis. We model the log stream as a directed event-transition network: Drain3 templates are vertices and successive co-occurrences are edges. A Block ID, time window, or client session is a walk on this graph. On HUFLIT-Career, client IPs define client-specific walks over the event-transition graph; IP identifiers are used only for grouping and are excluded from model features. Anomalies are walks that visit rare transitions or deviate quantitatively from normal traversal statistics.

This is an applied evaluation study, not a new detector. The template sequence after Drain3 is an intermediate layer: compact for lightweight baselines, structured for sequence models, and mappable to operational units. We ask: (RQ1)~how does post-Drain3 grouping affect the signal; (RQ2)~how do reconstruction, isolation, graph-walk NLL, next-event prediction, TCN, and masked modeling compare on the same walks; (RQ3)~does the ranking hold from LogHub benchmarks to an institutional web log.

\textbf{Contributions:} (1)~a leakage-aware pipeline that structures logs as walks on directed event-transition graphs; (2)~\emph{operational} GraphWalk scoring via Laplace-smoothed $P(v\mid u)$, alongside classical and deep branches; (3)~evaluation on HDFS, BGL, and HUFLIT-Career; (4)~deployment trade-offs (F1, latency, explainability) and explicit proxy-label limits.

\paragraph{Relation to prior manuscript.}
A related HDFS/BGL Drain3--TCN--Transformer manuscript was accepted at VNICT~2026 but subsequently withdrawn before publication~\cite{b26}. This CSoNet paper extends that manuscript with HUFLIT-Career, explicit transition-graph scoring, grouping/latency/explainability analyses, and clearer label/reproduction limits.

\section{Related Work}
Early statistical approaches include PCA and Isolation Forest~\cite{b10,b11}; recent empirical work~\cite{b24} shows classical models can remain competitive under particular log structures. Sequential models such as DeepLog~\cite{b1}, LogAnomaly~\cite{b13}, and LogRobust~\cite{b14} score event trajectories; PLELog~\cite{b15} reduces label cost. Drain/Drain3~\cite{b3} and LogPPT~\cite{b7} address parsing. Transformer approaches (LogBERT~\cite{b2}, surveys~\cite{b16}) build on self-attention~\cite{b4}. LLM/language-model methods include LogPrompt~\cite{b8}, LogGPT~\cite{b9}, LogLLM~\cite{b20}, CoLA~\cite{b21}, LogFiT~\cite{b22}, and LINEADAPTER~\cite{b23}; separately, LogAction~\cite{b25} targets cross-system transfer via active domain adaptation.

\textbf{Graph-based log anomaly detection.} LogGD~\cite{b27} and Logs2Graphs~\cite{b28} use GNN stacks over log graphs. Our GraphWalk branch is lighter: a first-order Markov transition graph with NLL and fusion features.

Gaps we target: missing order in classical methods; sequential bottlenecks; parser coupling; and under-specified grouping. We standardize parse$\rightarrow$group$\rightarrow$score with Drain3 and evaluate BoE baselines, GraphWalk, DeepLog-style LSTM, TCN, and Transformer (5 seeds). Code/dumps: \url{https://github.com/thtcsec/log-anomaly-tcn-transformer}.

\section{Proposed Methodology}
\subsection{Pipeline Overview}
Input: raw logs $L=\{l_1,\ldots,l_N\}$. Output: anomaly score $a(S)$ per walk $S$ (HDFS: Block ID; BGL: window; HUFLIT: client IP window). Six steps: (1)~collect; (2)~normalize variables; (3)~Drain3 parse to event IDs (vertices $V$); (4)~group into walks; (5)~represent walks as BoE/TF-IDF, as GraphWalk transition features, or as raw event sequences; (6)~score with reconstruction, Isolation Forest, GraphWalk NLL, or sequence models (Fig.~\ref{fig_pipeline}).

\subsection{Parsing, Grouping, and Classical Baselines}
Drain3 maps each line to a template via token similarity $Sim(T_1,T_2)$ with threshold $st{=}0.5$ (depth 4). HDFS groups by Block ID; BGL uses non-overlapping $W{=}100$ lines. PCA/TruncatedSVD use BoE counts with reconstruction error and a 95th-percentile threshold on normal training scores ($20$ components). Isolation Forest uses TF-IDF with $200$ trees, fit on normal sequences only. \textbf{Primary IF protocol:} threshold $-\texttt{decision\_function}$ at the 95th percentile of normal training scores (same family as PCA/GraphWalk). Setting \texttt{contamination} to the clipped training anomaly rate is a \emph{label-informed prevalence} calibration and is archived under \texttt{logs/legacy\_contamination/}, not used as the unsupervised headline.

\subsection{Event-Transition Graph Scoring (GraphWalk)}
\label{sec:graphwalk}
From \emph{normal} training walks we build a directed first-order Markov graph $G=(V,E)$ with Laplace-smoothed transitions
\begin{equation}
P(v\mid u)=\frac{c(u,v)+\alpha}{c(u)+\alpha|V|},\quad \alpha{=}1.
\end{equation}
Vertices are Drain3 event IDs only; padding tokens are excluded from graph construction and scoring. Each test walk yields mean/worst-edge transition NLL, unseen-edge and novel-node ratios, unique-event ratio, and length. We evaluate GraphWalk mean-NLL (95th-percentile on normal train) and late fusion of $z$-scored GraphWalk NLL with $z$-scored TF-IDF Isolation Forest scores.

\subsection{Deep Sequence Models}
\textbf{DeepLog-style LSTM:} next-event prediction with a 2-layer LSTM inspired by DeepLog~\cite{b1}, not a bit-exact Spell/full-corpus reproduction. Top-$k$ flags a sequence if any event falls outside the top $K$; NLL uses a 95th-percentile threshold on normal validation. \textbf{TCN:} dilated causal convolutions~\cite{b19} with the same split/scoring protocol. \textbf{Transformer:} masked event modeling~\cite{b2,b4} (mask ratio $0.15$), score $=$ mean MLM loss. Optimizer: AdamW~\cite{b17}.

\textbf{Prefix sizes (artifact-matched).} Classical HDFS/BGL baselines use a 500k-line LogHub prefix. On HDFS, DeepLog-style LSTM, TCN, Transformer, GraphWalk, and ablations use a 200k-line prefix. On BGL, PCA/SVD/IF/Transformer/DeepLog/TCN use 500k lines (\texttt{logs/bgl\_results.json}), whereas GraphWalk and the grouping study use a 200k-line subset. Neither setting is the full HDFS\_v1 corpus ($>11$M lines), so these values are not bit-exact reproductions of published HDFS results (different parsers, corpus sizes, sequence construction, or splits).

\subsection{Datasets}

\section{Results and Discussion}
\subsection{Cross-Dataset Comparison}
Table~\ref{tab_cross_dataset}: no single winner across protocols. On HDFS, PCA achieves $0.5711\pm0.0944$ (500k classical), while DeepLog-style and TCN both obtain $0.4613\pm0.0199$ (200k deep; identical per-seed top-$k$). This equality follows from the coarse sequence-level top-$K$ rule rather than identical model outputs: at $K{=}9$, both yield the same binary any-mismatch flags on evaluated HDFS sequences, while NLL scores differ. Prefix sizes also differ, so classical vs.\ deep HDFS F1 is not a head-to-head ranking. On BGL (500k except GraphWalk), TCN top-$k$ ($0.9826\pm0.0022$) is comparable to DeepLog-style ($0.9820\pm0.0042$). On HUFLIT-Career, Isolation Forest leads classical/graph scorers ($0.702\pm0.023$), with GraphWalk $0.647\pm0.070$ and PCA $0.592\pm0.137$; DeepLog/TCN NLL remain $0.592\pm0.010$ under the historical deep dump.

\subsection{Isolation Forest Variance}
Under the unified 95th-percentile protocol, IF F1 remains low on HDFS ($0.038\pm0.017$) and BGL ($0.017\pm0.006$) but moderate on Career. Career anomalies are scanner-like in a $\sim$6-template space; HDFS/BGL anomalies often share frequent templates. BGL OR-window labels mix normal and anomalous lines in one bag, collapsing isolation cuts while sequence NLL/top-$k$ still fire locally.

\subsection{Grouping (RQ1) and Ablations}
On BGL 200k, $W\in\{50,100,200\}$ and sliding $W{=}100$/stride $50$ (Table~\ref{tab_grouping}; 3 epochs, 5 seeds): PCA is sensitive to $W$; DeepLog-style/TCN remain robust; sliding yields the strongest sequence F1 (TCN $0.9106\pm0.0181$).

\textbf{Threats:} heuristic HUFLIT labels; Drain3 $\theta$/grouping; HDFS deep models use 200k prefix; Drain3/vocabulary on evaluated prefix before split (transductive, unlabeled); withdrawn VNICT manuscript~\cite{b26} overlaps the HDFS/BGL backbone.

\section{Conclusion}
We evaluated a Drain3 pipeline that uses an event-transition graph (GraphWalk) alongside classical and deep scorers. No branch dominates: PCA remains competitive under the classical HDFS protocol; TCN and DeepLog-style are nearly tied on BGL; under Career weak labels, percentile IF obtains the highest mean F1 among regenerated unsupervised scorers, with GraphWalk providing complementary transition signal without material AUROC gain in fusion. Choose scorers by vocabulary size, walk length, and label semantics. Future work: gold SOC labels, GNN embeddings, train-only parser/vocabulary construction, regenerated HUFLIT deep dumps under the no-PAD walk construction, and matched-prefix HDFS protocols.
