\begin{abstract}
System and web logs record both infrastructure state and user behavior, yet rule-based detectors age quickly and many learning methods either ignore event order or compare scorers under incompatible protocols. Casting parsed templates as nodes of a transition network makes that structure explicit: a block, a time window, or a client session is a walk, not a bag of events.
We present a network-aware log anomaly detection pipeline that treats Drain3 templates as vertices of a directed transition graph and each log sequence as a walk on that graph. Walks are scored with explicit transition-probability features (GraphWalk) alongside PCA/SVD, Isolation Forest, a DeepLog-style LSTM, TCN, and a masked Transformer. PCA remains strong under the HDFS classical protocol; TCN and DeepLog-style LSTM are nearly tied on BGL; on an institutional web log, method behavior depends on grouping and weak-label semantics. The study pairs GraphWalk with grouping, latency, and label-limitation analyses.

\keywords{Log anomaly detection \and event-transition network \and Transformer \and Drain3 \and AIOps.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Modern infrastructures generate diverse logs that capture operational state and behavioral patterns. Rule-based detectors age poorly; effective detection requires sequence-level analysis. We model the log stream as a directed event-transition network: Drain3 templates are vertices and successive co-occurrences are edges. A Block ID, time window, or client session is a walk on this graph. On HUFLIT-Career, client IPs define client-specific walks over the event-transition graph; IP identifiers are used only for grouping and are excluded from model features. Anomalies are walks that visit rare transitions or deviate quantitatively from normal traversal statistics.

This is primarily an applied evaluation study rather than a proposal of a new end-to-end detector architecture. The template sequence after Drain3 is an intermediate layer: compact for lightweight baselines, structured for sequence models, and mappable to operational units. We ask: (RQ1)~how does post-Drain3 grouping affect the signal; (RQ2)~how do reconstruction, isolation, graph-walk NLL, next-event prediction, TCN, and masked modeling compare on the same walks; (RQ3)~how do method behaviors and failure modes change from LogHub benchmarks to an institutional web log.

\textbf{Contributions:} (1)~a Drain3 walk pipeline with client-disjoint (HUFLIT) and chronological (BGL grouping) splits before windowing; (2)~\emph{operational} GraphWalk scoring via Laplace-smoothed $P(v\mid u)$ over $V\cup\{\mathrm{UNK}\}$, alongside classical and deep branches; (3)~evaluation on HDFS, BGL, and HUFLIT-Career; (4)~deployment trade-offs and proxy-label limits.

\section{Related Work}
Early statistical approaches include PCA and Isolation Forest~\cite{b10,b11}; empirical work~\cite{b24} shows classical models can remain competitive. Sequential models such as DeepLog~\cite{b1}, LogAnomaly~\cite{b13}, and LogRobust~\cite{b14} score event trajectories. Drain/Drain3~\cite{b3} address parsing; Transformers (LogBERT~\cite{b2}, surveys~\cite{b16}) build on self-attention~\cite{b4}. Recent LLM-oriented detectors~\cite{b20,b22} and GNN log graphs~\cite{b27,b28} are complementary to our lighter GraphWalk branch.

Gaps we target: missing order in classical methods; sequential bottlenecks; and under-specified grouping. We standardize parse$\rightarrow$group$\rightarrow$score with Drain3 and evaluate BoE baselines, GraphWalk, DeepLog-style LSTM, TCN, and Transformer (5 seeds). Public code: \path{github.com/thtcsec/log-anomaly-tcn-transformer}.

\section{Proposed Methodology}
\subsection{Pipeline Overview}
Input: raw logs $L=\{l_1,\ldots,l_N\}$. Output: anomaly score $a(S)$ per walk $S$ (HDFS: Block ID; BGL: window; HUFLIT: client IP window). Six steps: (1)~collect; (2)~normalize variables; (3)~Drain3 parse to event IDs (vertices $V$); (4)~group into walks; (5)~represent walks as BoE/TF-IDF, as GraphWalk transition features, or as raw event sequences; (6)~score with reconstruction, Isolation Forest, GraphWalk NLL, or sequence models (Fig.~\ref{fig_pipeline}).

\subsection{Parsing, Grouping, and Classical Baselines}
Drain3 maps each line to a template via token similarity $Sim(T_1,T_2)$ with threshold $st{=}0.5$ (depth 4). HDFS groups by Block ID; BGL uses non-overlapping $W{=}100$ lines. PCA/TruncatedSVD use BoE counts with reconstruction error and a 95th-percentile threshold on normal training scores ($20$ components). Isolation Forest uses TF-IDF with $200$ trees, fit on normal sequences only. \textbf{Primary IF protocol:} threshold $-\texttt{decision\_function}$ at the 95th percentile of normal training scores (same family as PCA/GraphWalk). Setting \texttt{contamination} to the clipped training anomaly rate is a \emph{label-informed prevalence} calibration and is archived under \texttt{logs/legacy\_contamination/}, not used as the unsupervised headline.

\subsection{Event-Transition Graph Scoring (GraphWalk)}
\label{sec:graphwalk}
From \emph{normal} training walks we build a directed first-order Markov graph $G=(V,E)$ with Laplace-smoothed transitions over $V\cup\{\mathrm{UNK}\}$ ($\alpha{=}1$):
\begin{equation}
P(v\mid u)=\frac{c(u,v)+\alpha}{c(u)+\alpha(|V|{+}1)}.
\end{equation}
Novel destinations map to $\mathrm{UNK}$; unseen sources use uniform $1/(|V|{+}1)$. Vertices are Drain3 IDs only (no padding). Each test walk yields mean/worst-edge NLL, unseen-edge and novel-node ratios, unique-event ratio, and length. We report GraphWalk mean-NLL (95th-percentile on normal train) and late fusion with $z$-scored TF-IDF Isolation Forest.

\subsection{Deep Sequence Models}
\textbf{DeepLog-style LSTM:} next-event prediction with a 2-layer LSTM inspired by DeepLog~\cite{b1}, not a bit-exact Spell/full-corpus reproduction. Top-$k$ flags a sequence if any event falls outside the top $K$; NLL uses a 95th-percentile threshold on normal validation. \textbf{TCN:} dilated causal convolutions~\cite{b19} with the same split/scoring protocol. \textbf{Transformer:} masked event modeling~\cite{b2,b4} (mask ratio $0.15$), score $=$ mean MLM loss. Optimizer: AdamW~\cite{b17}.

\textbf{Prefix sizes (artifact-matched).} Classical HDFS/BGL baselines use a 500k-line LogHub prefix. On HDFS, neural models and GraphWalk use a 200k-line prefix. On BGL, classical and deep scorers use 500k lines; GraphWalk and the grouping study use 200k. Neither is the full HDFS\_v1 corpus ($>11$M lines), so results are not bit-exact reproductions of published HDFS numbers (parser, corpus size, sequence construction, and splits differ).

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Datasets}
HDFS and BGL are from LogHub~\cite{b6,b12}. HUFLIT-Career is a one-month (June 2026) institutional HTTP log; IPs and payloads are hashed. Client IPs are grouping keys only, never features. \textbf{Heuristic/weak labels:} scanner-probe labels come from fixed export rules on \texttt{suspicious\_signals}, not adjudicated SOC ground truth; metrics measure agreement with the heuristic. \section{Results and Discussion}
\subsection{Cross-Dataset Comparison}
Table~\ref{tab_cross_dataset}: no single winner across protocols. On HDFS, PCA achieves $0.5711\pm0.0944$ (500k classical), while DeepLog-style and TCN both obtain $0.4613\pm0.0199$ (200k deep; identical per-seed top-$k$). This equality follows from the coarse sequence-level top-$K$ rule rather than identical model outputs: at $K{=}9$, both yield the same binary any-mismatch flags on evaluated HDFS sequences, while NLL scores differ. Prefix sizes also differ, so classical vs.\ deep HDFS F1 is not a head-to-head ranking. On BGL (500k except GraphWalk), TCN top-$k$ ($0.9826\pm0.0022$) is comparable to DeepLog-style ($0.9820\pm0.0042$). On HUFLIT-Career (client-disjoint), Isolation Forest leads regenerated unsupervised scorers ($0.539\pm0.298$) but with high seed variance; GraphWalk $0.496\pm0.270$ and PCA $0.458\pm0.263$; DeepLog/TCN NLL remain $0.592\pm0.010$ under the historical deep dump.

\subsection{Isolation Forest Variance}
Under the unified 95th-percentile protocol, IF F1 remains low on HDFS ($0.038\pm0.017$) and BGL ($0.017\pm0.006$) but moderate-mean on Career under client-disjoint splits ($0.539\pm0.298$), with large seed variance. Career anomalies are scanner-like in a small template space; HDFS/BGL anomalies often share frequent templates. BGL OR-window labels mix normal and anomalous lines in one bag, collapsing isolation cuts while sequence NLL/top-$k$ still fire locally.

\subsection{Grouping (RQ1) and Ablations}
On BGL 200k, $W\in\{50,100,200\}$ and sliding $W{=}100$/stride $50$ (Table~\ref{tab_grouping}; chronological split before windowing; 3 epochs, 5 seeds): absolute F1 drops versus the older window-level random split, but ranking is unchanged---PCA is sensitive to $W$, sequence models lead, and sliding remains strongest (TCN $0.482\pm0.019$).

\section{Conclusion}
We evaluated a Drain3 pipeline with GraphWalk alongside classical and deep scorers. No branch dominates across protocols and label regimes. Choose scorers by vocabulary size, walk length, and label semantics. Future work: gold SOC labels, GNN embeddings, train-only parser construction, and matched-prefix HDFS protocols.
